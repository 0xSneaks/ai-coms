## Platform facts

### OpenCode Go (opencode.ai)
- **Plans:** Go $10/month, Go Plus $40/month, Go Teams $20/member/month. Source: https://opencode.ai/v2/docs/console/go
- **How the allowance works:** each model has its own **monthly dollar cap** on the plan (e.g. Muse Spark 1.2/1.3 Contributor $60/month on Go; free models such as Beam Free and Iceman Contributor Free are "Unlimited" for a limited time). Usage is metered against that cap at the model's published per-token price. Rolling windows sit on top: the 5-hour window allows up to 20% of the monthly cap, the weekly window up to 50%, the monthly window 100%. Go Teams gives each member their own limits and adds $5/model to caps vs Go. Source: https://opencode.ai/v2/docs/console/go
- **OpenCode's own handling:** OpenCode's legal privacy policy (effective 2026-03-06) lists prompt/conversation content as "passing through to upstream provider to provide services" and **"Not stored"** by OpenCode itself — retention/training is decided by the upstream model provider, which is why the per-model table below governs. Source: https://opencode.ai/legal/privacy-policy
- **Per-model privacy (official Go privacy table):** most Go models are "Data used for training: Not used" with **0 days** retention (zero data retention). Exceptions on the current roster:
  - **Muse Spark 1.2 Contributor and 1.3 Contributor: training = Yes, retention = "Not ZDR."** The docs state the discount is "in exchange for permission to use your prompts and completions to train future Meta models." These two models also require the workspace setting "Allow paid endpoints that train on request data" to be explicitly enabled — OpenCode's workspace privacy defaults otherwise block paid endpoints that may train on request data.
  - **Iceman Contributor Free: training = Yes, retention = "Not ZDR"** ("free for a limited time in exchange for permission to use your prompts and completions to improve the model").
  - **Beam Free: training = Not used, retention = 30 days** (the 30-day figure is confirmed on the official Go page).
  - A few non-training models also retain 30 days (e.g. Grok 4.7, GPT-5.6/6 Luna, Claude Haiku 5.5 per the same table).
  - DeepSeek V4 Pro / V4.1 Flash on Go: Not used, 0 days, under a ZDR agreement with DeepSeek that OpenCode notes is "renewed monthly (current agreement valid through November 30, 2026)."
  Source: https://opencode.ai/v2/docs/console/go (Privacy section)
- **"Contributor" tier, defined by OpenCode:** models offered at heavily discounted (or free) prices specifically because the user grants the maker permission to train on prompts and completions. Both Muse Spark Contributor models and Iceman Contributor Free sit in this tier; they are the only training-permitted models in the Go lineup researched here.

### OpenRouter — free models
- **OpenRouter itself:** per its privacy policy (updated 2026-08-31) and privacy docs, OpenRouter **does not use Inputs or Outputs for training**, and **does not store prompts or responses unless the user opts into prompt logging**; request metadata is always retained. Source: https://openrouter.ai/privacy · https://openrouter.ai/docs/features/privacy-and-logging
- **Providers are the variable:** "Some Model Providers may use your Inputs and Outputs for model training or improvement." Every endpoint carries a public data-policy label (training yes/no, retention period). On the free tier specifically, labels seen in this pass range from "Retained for 30 days | Does not train" (Cohere) to "retained for unknown period | May train" (Thinking Machines) and Liquid's own "may be retained and used to train Liquid models."
- **User controls:** account privacy settings have **separate training opt-out toggles for paid and free models** — enabling them stops routing to providers that may train; API parameters `provider.data_collection: "deny"` and `zdr: true` restrict routing per request. Source: https://openrouter.ai/docs/features/privacy-and-logging
- **Free-tier limits (official docs):** 20 requests/minute; 50 requests/day if under $10 lifetime credits purchased, 1,000/day at $10+. Source: https://openrouter.ai/docs/features/privacy-and-logging (limits also stated in OpenRouter's docs/FAQ)

### Ollama Cloud (ollama.com)
- **Official privacy policy:** models run locally collect nothing. For **cloud-hosted models, Ollama processes prompts and responses transiently to provide the service, does not store them beyond the time needed to fulfill the request, and never trains on them** ("we process your prompts and responses transiently to provide the service and never train on it"). What Ollama does collect is account information and limited usage metadata that does not include prompt/response content. No fixed day-count retention applies to prompt content because it isn't stored. Source: https://ollama.com/privacy

### DeepSeek — direct API (deepseek.com)
- **One privacy policy covers the API and the apps** (last updated 2026-02-10). Its text, as published by DeepSeek: data is collected, processed and **stored in the People's Republic of China**; user inputs/personal data are kept **for as long as the account is active / as long as necessary** — no fixed day count is published; stated purposes include using inputs **"to improve and develop the Services and to train and improve our technology, such as our machine learning models and algorithms,"** and the policy describes a right to opt out of training. The Open Platform Terms of Service (effective 2026-04-29) contain no separate retention clause for API inputs/outputs and defer personal-data handling to the privacy policy — i.e. **no API-specific training carve-out and no ZDR program**; training is on by default for first-party API traffic unless the user opts out. One narrow exception in the official API docs: on-disk context caching is cleared "usually within a few hours to a few days." Sources: https://cdn.deepseek.com/policies/en-US/deepseek-privacy-policy.html · https://api-docs.deepseek.com (context caching)
- **Contrast that matters for the rundown:** the same DeepSeek models reached **through OpenCode Go** sit under OpenCode's ZDR agreement (0 days, no training, per the Go table above) — the direct-API terms do not apply to Go traffic.
